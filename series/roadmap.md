# Reflections: Connected Series Roadmap

Recorded: 2026-11-12.
Status: User-supplied series direction and DRAFT future story plan. No new
chapter events, resolutions, art, or release approved by this document.

## One Continuous Family Story

These are 15 interconnected stories within one Reflections continuity, not
15 separate relationships or alternate versions of the cast. Ethan experiences
different sides of love, power, affection, and conflict through his marriages
to Morgan and Ruby. All three have agency and their own character journeys.

Central series question:

> How can someone be the person everyone needs, without losing the person they actually are?

Use the six S-tier concepts as the main emotional framework. The A-tier
concepts develop the characters and the B-tier concepts focus symbolic or
specific emotional moments. Tiers indicate narrative priority, not canon
status, quality approval, chapter count, or publication order.

## Core Character Direction

| Character | Marriage dynamic | Internal tension |
|---|---|---|
| Ethan | More submissive with Morgan, more dominant with Ruby; can feel overwhelmed in the former dynamic and confident/protective in the latter | Feels responsible for others' happiness while struggling to express needs and preserve his own identity |
| Morgan | Dominant, strong-willed, passionate, and confrontational with Ethan | Wants independence and control while also needing support, understanding, tenderness, and love |
| Ruby | More submissive with Ethan; nurturing, affectionate, emotionally attentive | Wants harmony and closeness but struggles to voice needs and may resent always keeping the peace |

These roles are consensual adult relationship dynamics. They do not decide
who gets a voice, who can refuse, or who is responsible for the family.
Ethan needs agency, Morgan needs tenderness, and Ruby needs boundaries.
Ethan contributes to conflicts; neither woman exists solely to cause or soothe them.
Morgan and Ruby's independent friendship remains close, meaningful, and platonic.

## Interconnection Rules

- Carry approved relationship changes, boundaries, fatigue, trust, and attempts
  at repair into later chapters. Do not reset the marriage after each story.
- Use earlier disagreements and small changes as context for later choices.
  Specific callbacks are chosen during scripting, not invented as past canon.
- Show Morgan and Ruby talking, disagreeing, supporting one another, and making
  parenting decisions without Ethan present. Their tenderness is platonic.
- Give the children ongoing interests, family bonds, and viewpoints. Preserve
  Rowan/Riley's close bond and Avery's connection with Ethan without reducing
  Avery to exclusion or additional support needs.
- Ruby is particularly attentive to Avery's support needs, while all adults
  share parenting and retain the established family attachments and work roles.
- Keep the same series identity question active while letting each story have
  its own visible action, emotional turn, and partial consequence.

## Continuity Decisions Before Scripting

| Concept | Decision to resolve |
|---|---|
| The Weight of Everything | Establish why the load changes from the existing full-time/part-time/daily-care baseline; do not erase anyone's work |
| Words That Never Arrived | How fictional writings are encountered and shared, with privacy and consent addressed |
| The Empty Side of the Bed | Scene-specific sleeping arrangements; no permanent bedroom layout established |
| The Person I Used to Be | What fictional past harm occurred; no specific incident is approved by this concept |
| The Ring in the Drawer | Current absence versus flashback; motivation, location, and timing remain draft details |
| After the Fire | Adult consent and accountability; passion does not prove safety or repair harm |
| The Distance Between Us | Emergency type and negotiated practical connection; privacy is not disloyalty |
| One Foot Out the Door / Choose Me Again | Specific choices and consequences; no separation or complete reconciliation has happened yet |

## Chapter Scope

Each concept may become one short story or several chapters. The supplied
example for The Weight of Everything is a three-part proposal of 18-24 pages
per part, 54-72 pages total; it does not fix the length of every other story.
Its full example is saved in [story 003](../stories/arc-02/concepts/story-003-the-weight-of-everything.md).

The earlier short-pilot recommendation remains a production option, not a
replacement for this longer example or a selected standard. Global chapter
IDs and page counts are assigned when concepts are expanded. Chapter 001's
existing planning scaffold is preserved; no script or artwork is created here.
